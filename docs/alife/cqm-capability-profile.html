CQM-25: the project capability standard.

A research-informed profile for artificial life, embodied synthetic cognition and procedural morphogenesis, spanning the organization of one being, adaptive behavior, consciousness-related mechanisms and the engineering of inspectable worlds.

Version 1.0.0 · adopted 2026-10-22. These 25 operational dimensions govern project capability plans and claims across individual beings, interacting populations and constructed worlds.

Canonical project authority; explicit evidence limits

CQM-25 is the binding project standard, not a consensus certification or an executed 25-task benchmark. Acceptance experiments remain proposed and unexecuted as a unified benchmark. Review states describe available repository evidence. Numerical capability scores remain uncalibrated.

Feature plans and capability claims must identify the affected stable axis IDs and their evidence requirements. Taxonomy changes require an explicit framework version and contract review.

Engineering breadth and visual expression do not count as evidence of consciousness, sentience, AGI or superintelligence. No aggregate verdict is calculated.

The capability law

Adopted 2026-10-22 as the binding capability framework of this repository (ADR 0018). It governs claims made here; it is not an external consensus standard.

  1. CQM-25 is the canonical capability framework of this repository. Every claim about life-like organization, cognition, consciousness-linked mechanisms, valence-related control or engineering scale, in documentation, user interface text, commits and agent output, is made against it.
  2. A capability claim names its CQM-25 axis and that axis's current evidence state, and claims no more than that state supports.
  3. An evidence state changes only with repository evidence that exists at the pinned commit and a passing test. Raising a state follows the axis's acceptance experiment and survives its falsifier.
  4. Numeric capability scores stay null until a calibrated successor edition executes the axis protocols with controls, independent experimental units and reported rater agreement.
  5. The nine-axis, 155-system peer survey is a legacy comparison view. It keeps its own rubric and scores, is crosswalked to CQM-25 at the level of constructs only, and never supplies or receives CQM-25 scores. Construct correspondence does not imply that the legacy survey measured or validated CQM-25 acceptance protocols.
  6. No aggregate life, consciousness, sentience, AGI or superintelligence score is derived from the axes.
  7. Separately labeled provisional author estimates may use the declared uncalibrated heuristic rubric in cqm-capability-estimates.json. They remain pending-review proposals, never populate canonical assessment.score and supply no aggregate or domain mean.

Scope of the standard

Constructed physics is evaluated against declared rules and constraints. Ecology is one domain among several. An entity-level study can declare reproduction or population ecology not applicable, with a reason; a missing measurement remains unknown.

Historical nine-axis comparison and visual review

The existing visual-scale rating remains 4.0 (rich visuals). A single complex being qualifies: Xylos exposes neural activity through deforming membranes and a visible graph, while apex forms and populations add other scales. Population capacity, delivered frame time and individual visual expressiveness are distinct measurements. The rubric's 5.0 anchor, iconic spectacle, has no independent calibration that justifies a numerical uplift in this review.

The original nine-axis, 155-system comparison is historical secondary context. It retains its own rubric and scores; its rankings and averages are not transferred into the canonical CQM-25 standard.

01life · Life, evolution and embodiment

Reproduction and heredity

Production of descendants with causally inherited, variable organization.

reproduction_heredityentity / populationBounded test evidenceLegacy construct correspondence: Reproduction

Genome crossover and mutation are implemented, and the shipped entity fission test compares inherited-trait selection with a trait-blind control. This supports bounded heredity and fecundity selection; autonomous open-ended reproduction and the new pedigree protocol remain unassessed.

Capability score: uncalibrated

Measures, protocol and evidence

Record these measures

  • viable offspring per parent
  • parent-offspring trait transmission
  • heritable variation under matched conditions

Acceptance experiment

Track pedigrees across generations; intervene on inherited state, randomize parent-offspring assignments as a control, and distinguish autonomous reproduction from evaluator cloning.

What would weaken the claim?

Offspring are injected by the harness, inherited variables do not affect descendants, or transmission vanishes after environment matching.

Repository evidence

  • Genome representation, crossover and mutation src/sim/genome.ts
  • Deterministic inheritance and variation tests tests/genome.test.ts
  • Production fission versus trait-blind selection control tests/entity-repro-select.test.ts

What a strong benchmark result must report

  1. Feature plans and capability claims identify affected stable CQM-25 axis IDs and their evidence requirements. Taxonomy changes require an explicit framework version and contract review; evidence updates preserve axis identity and pinned provenance.
  2. Declare the unit of analysis, entity boundary, intended modeling fidelity and applicable axes before evaluation. Treat individual, population and world measurements separately. N/A needs a scope reason; missing or failed evidence cannot be relabeled N/A.
  3. Freeze the implementation, task generator, development and held-out distributions, training exposure, horizon, hardware and resource budget. Preregister primary metrics, meaningful effect sizes, failure conditions and stopping rules.
  4. Use controls appropriate to the claim: random/fixed policy, frozen learning, interrupted recurrence, disconnected broadcast, replayed environment or matched sham intervention. Match compute and other confounds where feasible.
  5. Report independent experimental units and their dependence. Consecutive frames are not independent replicates; population studies must account for shared lineages and use independently initialized runs.
  6. Publish outcome distributions, uncertainty intervals, negative results, missingness and sensitivity analyses. Use multiplicity control for families of inferential tests; do not select only favorable seeds.
  7. Archive the commit, configuration, seeds or recorded entropy inputs, interventions and raw outputs. Reproduce each result within stated numerical tolerances, and seek independent replication of the specific result.
  8. Separate capability measurement, implementation evidence, causal evidence and external validity. A unit-test pass or a named module does not establish the full axis protocol. Strong evidence can support a negative result.
  9. Before cross-system scoring, calibrate task-specific anchors on multiple systems, use independent raters and report agreement. Publish all eligible denominators and resource limits. Do not translate nine historical scores into 25 new scores.
  10. For AGI or superintelligence comparisons, declare task-family coverage, capability depth, reference populations, adaptation budget, tool access, compute, time and human assistance. Report weak domains; neither a single exceptional task nor a profile average establishes general superhuman ability.
  11. For neurometric analogues, identify the estimator, perturbation design, null models and validation domain. Human PCI thresholds, brain-score correlations and animal sentience criteria do not automatically transfer to software.
  12. Reuse relevant existing evaluation resources such as Avida, MODES, EvoGym, Animal-AI, Melting Pot and Brain-Score with their own protocols. This profile organizes several research goals; it is not the first ALife benchmark or a universal standard.

Limits of this edition

  • Project adoption on 2026-10-22 establishes CQM-25 as the canonical framework. It does not rerun the 2026-10-21 evidence review: its 25 acceptance protocols remain proposed and unexecuted as a unified benchmark; every numeric capability score is null.
  • The selected axes and grouping are author-defined, correlated and not exhaustive. Primary literature motivates the constructs; it does not establish consensus on this exact profile or equal axis weights.
  • Implementation and bounded-test labels refer to cited repository mechanisms and narrow tests. Failed V4 and Phase-B advancement criteria remain visible; mechanism presence does not imply generalization.
  • Life-like organization, cognition, subjective consciousness and valenced sentience are different claims. Theory-linked mechanisms and reward or valence variables do not establish experience.
  • The profile supplements the original Butlin indicator mapping; it does not replace those 14 indicators or claim their criteria are all met. Human neuroscience and animal behavior evidence require justified transfer to artificial systems.
  • Engineering breadth, substrate count, photorealism, visual beauty, number of beings and parameter count contribute no points to mental-capability claims. No overall life, consciousness, sentience, AGI or SI score is calculated.
  • The separate provisional author estimates use an uncalibrated heuristic implementation rubric. Their imported rationales await independent review and are not new protocol results; they never populate canonical capability scores or produce aggregate verdicts.

Research foundations

  1. Open problems in artificial life (research agenda)ALife spans origins, evolutionary potential, and relations to mind; this is an agenda, not a certification standard.
  2. The evolutionary origin of complex features (primary experiment)Digital replication, mutation and selection can be investigated experimentally; complexity is task-relative.
  3. The MODES Toolbox: Measurements of Open-Ended Dynamics in Evolving Systems (measurement framework)Tracks change, novelty, complexity and ecological potential. Finite observations cannot prove unlimited open-endedness.

  5. Growing Neural Cellular Automata (primary experiment)Demonstrates learned growth, persistence and regeneration; repair is distinct from subjective experience.
