Cosmogonic Quantum Mechalogodrom · proposed framework

Digital life, beyond a single axis.

A research-informed profile for artificial life, embodied synthetic cognition and procedural morphogenesis, spanning the organization of one being, adaptive behavior, consciousness-related mechanisms and the engineering of inspectable worlds.

25 operational dimensions. Individual beings, interacting populations and constructed worlds can be assessed at their declared scope.

A research profile, with evidence kept visible

These are proposed constructs and acceptance experiments, not a consensus certification or an executed 25-task benchmark. Review states describe available repository evidence. Numerical capability scores remain uncalibrated.

Engineering breadth and visual expression do not count as evidence of consciousness, sentience, AGI or superintelligence. No aggregate verdict is calculated.

Scope and the visual-scale review

The existing visual-scale rating remains 4.0 (rich visuals). A single complex being qualifies: Xylos exposes neural activity through deforming membranes and a visible graph, while apex forms and populations add other scales. Population capacity, delivered frame time and individual visual expressiveness are distinct measurements. The rubric's 5.0 anchor, iconic spectacle, has no independent calibration that justifies a numerical uplift in this review.

Constructed physics is evaluated against declared rules and constraints. Ecology is one domain among several. An entity-level study can declare reproduction or population ecology not applicable, with a reason; a missing measurement remains unknown.

The original nine-axis, 155-system comparison retains its own rubric and scores. Its rankings and averages are not transferred into this profile.

What a strong benchmark result must report

  1. Declare the unit of analysis, entity boundary, intended modeling fidelity and applicable axes before evaluation. Treat individual, population and world measurements separately. N/A needs a scope reason; missing or failed evidence cannot be relabeled N/A.
  2. Freeze the implementation, task generator, development and held-out distributions, training exposure, horizon, hardware and resource budget. Preregister primary metrics, meaningful effect sizes, failure conditions and stopping rules.
  3. Use controls appropriate to the claim: random/fixed policy, frozen learning, interrupted recurrence, disconnected broadcast, replayed environment or matched sham intervention. Match compute and other confounds where feasible.
  4. Report independent experimental units and their dependence. Consecutive frames are not independent replicates; population studies must account for shared lineages and use independently initialized runs.
  5. Publish outcome distributions, uncertainty intervals, negative results, missingness and sensitivity analyses. Use multiplicity control for families of inferential tests; do not select only favorable seeds.
  6. Archive the commit, configuration, seeds or recorded entropy inputs, interventions and raw outputs. Reproduce each result within stated numerical tolerances, and seek independent replication of the specific result.
  7. Separate capability measurement, implementation evidence, causal evidence and external validity. A unit-test pass or a named module does not establish the full axis protocol. Strong evidence can support a negative result.
  8. Before cross-system scoring, calibrate task-specific anchors on multiple systems, use independent raters and report agreement. Publish all eligible denominators and resource limits. Do not translate nine historical scores into 25 new scores.
  9. For AGI or superintelligence comparisons, declare task-family coverage, capability depth, reference populations, adaptation budget, tool access, compute, time and human assistance. Report weak domains; neither a single exceptional task nor a profile average establishes general superhuman ability.
  10. For neurometric analogues, identify the estimator, perturbation design, null models and validation domain. Human PCI thresholds, brain-score correlations and animal sentience criteria do not automatically transfer to software.
  11. Reuse relevant existing evaluation resources such as Avida, MODES, EvoGym, Animal-AI, Melting Pot and Brain-Score with their own protocols. This profile organizes several research goals; it is not the first ALife benchmark or a universal standard.

Limits of this edition

  • This initial edition is a source and repository review with proposed experiments. Its 25 acceptance protocols have not been executed as a unified benchmark; every numeric capability score is null.
  • The selected axes and grouping are author-defined, correlated and not exhaustive. Primary literature motivates the constructs; it does not establish consensus on this exact profile or equal axis weights.
  • Implementation and bounded-test labels refer to cited repository mechanisms and narrow tests. Failed V4 and Phase-B advancement criteria remain visible; mechanism presence does not imply generalization.
  • Life-like organization, cognition, subjective consciousness and valenced sentience are different claims. Theory-linked mechanisms and reward or valence variables do not establish experience.
  • The profile supplements the original Butlin indicator mapping; it does not replace those 14 indicators or claim their criteria are all met. Human neuroscience and animal behavior evidence require justified transfer to artificial systems.
  • Engineering breadth, substrate count, photorealism, visual beauty, number of beings and parameter count contribute no points to mental-capability claims. No overall life, consciousness, sentience, AGI or SI score is calculated.

Research foundations

These sources motivate individual constructs and evaluation methods. They do not endorse the axis selection or establish universal thresholds.

  1. Open problems in artificial life (research agenda)ALife spans origins, evolutionary potential, and relations to mind; this is an agenda, not a certification standard.
  2. The evolutionary origin of complex features (primary experiment)Digital replication, mutation and selection can be investigated experimentally; complexity is task-relative.
  3. The MODES Toolbox: Measurements of Open-Ended Dynamics in Evolving Systems (measurement framework)Tracks change, novelty, complexity and ecological potential. Finite observations cannot prove unlimited open-endedness.
  4. Evolution Gym: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Evolving Soft Robots (benchmark)NeurIPS 2021 benchmark, arXiv posted 2022. Measures joint body/controller optimization, not consciousness.
  5. Growing Neural Cellular Automata (primary experiment)Demonstrates learned growth, persistence and regeneration; repair is distinct from subjective experience.
  6. Homeostatic reinforcement learning for integrating reward collection and physiological stability (computational neuroscience model)Connects internal-state regulation and reward-driven behavior; a candidate model rather than a universal requirement.
  7. Overcoming catastrophic forgetting in neural networks (primary experiment)Measures retention of previously learned tasks across subsequent training.
  8. The Animal-AI Environment: Training and Testing Animal-Like Artificial Cognition (benchmark)Animal-cognition-inspired testing beyond training environments; no implication of sentience.
  9. Position: Levels of AGI for Operationalizing Progress on the Path to AGI (research framework)Separates performance, generality and autonomy. A proposed framework, not a universally accepted AGI certificate.
  10. Measuring Progress Toward AGI: A Cognitive Framework (preprint framework)Proposes held-out cognitive tasks and cognitive profiles; May 2026 preprint, not established consensus.
  11. Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness (theory-derived research framework)Computational indicators drawn from several theories; meeting indicators is not proof of consciousness.
  12. Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems (methodological research)Theory-derived indicators address uncertainty and gaming; criteria remain revisable.
  13. Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness (preregistered primary experiment)Human neuroscience results challenged important predictions of both theories; do not portray either as settled consensus.
  14. A theoretically based index of consciousness independent of sensory processing and behavior (primary neurophysiology experiment)Human TMS/EEG perturbational complexity index; software analogues do not inherit human thresholds or diagnostic validity.
  15. How to measure metacognition (measurement methodology)Distinguishes metacognitive sensitivity, bias and efficiency; match first-order performance before comparison.
  16. Review of the Evidence of Sentience in Cephalopod Molluscs and Decapod Crustaceans (commissioned evidence review)Animal neural and behavioral indicators motivate converging evidence; no validated transfer to software.
  17. Motivational trade-offs and modulation of nociception in bumblebees (primary animal experiment)Context-dependent trade-offs motivate functional assays; reward variables and avoidance alone do not establish felt valence.
  18. Improving Reproducibility in Machine Learning Research (research methodology)Supports explicit code, data, experimental configuration and reproducibility reporting.
  19. Lenia - Biology of Artificial Life (ALife research platform)Continuous cellular automata support varied organism-like patterns; morphology alone does not establish cognition.
  20. Brain-Score: Which Artificial Neural Network for Object Recognition is most Brain-Like? (neural and behavioral benchmark)Neural/behavioral similarity is domain-specific; neither brain similarity nor a composite is a consciousness measure.
  21. Robots that can adapt like animals (primary robotics experiment)Tests recovery of function after unseen damage; distinguishes functional adaptation from structural regrowth.
  22. Scalable Evaluation of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Melting Pot (benchmark)Tests social interaction generalization to novel partners and situations.
  23. Autopoiesis: The organization of living systems, its characterization and a model (theoretical model)One influential account of maintained organization; not a settled universal definition of life.
